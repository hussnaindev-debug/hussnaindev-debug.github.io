The game

Bloom Prison is a roguelike action-adventure experience for Roblox, and it's the project I'm proudest of. I'm the sole developer on it at my company, and it's currently in early development. Every system, from combat and enemy AI to points of interest, cutscenes, abilities, VFX, and inventory, is designed and scripted by me in Roblox Studio with Luau.

Each run drops the player into a world of readable, reactive encounters and randomized points of interest, built around combat that stays fluid from one attack to the next. The sections below break down the core systems as they stand today, with much more planned as development continues.

What I built

Free-flow combat

Input-buffered melee that keeps the flow going from one attack to the next, with auto-targeting of the nearest enemy so mobile players never fight the controls.

Weapons & abilities

Each weapon has its own abilities, VFX, and clear strengths and weaknesses. Some abilities are pure attacks, others grant temporary buffs.

Distinct enemy AI

Every enemy is unique. Swarms, poisonous types, and ranged attackers each behave differently and have to be dealt with on their own terms.

Abilities & VFX

An ability system paired with a hand-built VFX layer, giving each power a distinct look and impactful on-screen feel.

Randomized POIs

Points of interest spawn randomly each run. Some grant armor, some weapons, some heal, driving exploration and replayability.

Cutscenes

Story cutscenes authored in Moon Animator, played back for every player in the server when triggered. One today, with more planned.

Inventory system & UI

A full inventory system backing a clean inventory UI, letting players manage weapons, items, and gear.

Roguelike runs

Randomized layouts and rewards each run give the game a repeatable, run-based structure built to keep encounters fresh.

Combat

Combat is built around flow. The goal is to make chaining from one attack into the next feel effortless, so encounters read as continuous sequences rather than isolated swings. Input buffering is central: the player can queue their next input mid-attack and it lands as soon as the current move allows, keeping the rhythm unbroken and rewarding timing without demanding frame-perfect execution.

To keep things approachable, especially on mobile, the player automatically targets the nearest enemy without needing to hold directional input, an approach inspired by the free-flow combat of the Batman: Arkham games. This lets players focus on offense and positioning instead of fighting the camera or controls.

Each weapon carries its own identity through a unique set of abilities and VFX, along with clear strengths and weaknesses that make weapon choice meaningful. Some abilities are pure offense, while others grant temporary buffs, giving players room to develop their own playstyle across a run.

Enemy AI

Every enemy is designed to be a distinct threat rather than a reskin. The roster spans different archetypes, from swarming enemies that overwhelm through numbers, to poisonous enemies that punish careless positioning, to ranged attackers that force the player to close distance or take cover. Each type behaves differently and has to be approached on its own terms.

Because their behaviors differ, encounters become a matter of reading the field and adapting, deciding which threat to neutralize first and how to handle a mixed group. Enemies detect the player, close in, and commit to attacks with their own timing and spacing, so fights stay reactive instead of predictable.

The enemy AI is designed to scale with the roguelike structure of the game, so as new archetypes and combinations are added through development, encounters can be composed to keep each run fresh.

Points of Interest

Points of interest are the exploration backbone of each run. They're placed randomly across the map every time, so no two runs lay out the same, which reinforces the roguelike loop and keeps players moving and searching rather than following a fixed path.

Each point of interest behaves differently and offers its own reward. Some grant armor, some hand over new weapons, some heal the player, and others carry their own effects, so deciding which to seek out and when becomes part of the moment-to-moment strategy. As development continues, the pool will grow to add more variety and more meaningful decisions to every run.

Cutscenes

Cutscenes deliver the game's story and frame its key moments. They're authored in Moon Animator and play back for every player in the server when triggered, so story beats land as a shared, synchronized experience rather than a private one.

The game currently features a single cutscene, with more planned as development progresses and the narrative expands. The system is built so additional cutscenes can be added and triggered cleanly as the world and its story grow.

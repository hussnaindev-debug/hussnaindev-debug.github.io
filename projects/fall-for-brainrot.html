The game

Fall for Brainrot is a Roblox game in development that mixes a downhill runner with a collector economy. Players climb into a glass bubble, launch off a pad at the top of the hill, and roll down a long slope, building momentum, dodging obstacles, and collecting coins and gems. How far you get determines the Brainrot you earn.

Back at your base, the Brainrots you've collected go on display and generate passive income, which you reinvest into upgrades and rebirths. I'm designing and building every system myself, from the custom ball physics to the economy, the 100-character collection, leaderboards, and anti-cheat.

What I built

The full system set, from the custom ball physics to the collector economy and anti-cheat.

Custom ball physics

A two-body glass bubble (rolling collision shell + upright interior seat) driven by angular velocity, so momentum, slopes, and terrain feel right for free.

Launch & run loop

Auto-seat on the pad, arc-launch off the top, then roll the downhill slope with hybrid controls (auto-roll, strafe, brake) and a speed-reactive follow camera.

Durability & pickups

Bubble HP with staged cracks and a physics glass-shatter, plus coin and gem pickups and server-tracked distance that gates the Brainrot reward.

100 Brainrots

A config-driven collection of 100 animated characters across five rarities, with a collection index, a Tool-based inventory, and placement on your base.

Bases & passive income

Personal bases with display stands; the Brainrots you equip generate income per second that you walk over to collect.

Upgrades, rebirth & quests

Glass-strength, coin-multiplier, and gem-luck upgrades, a rebirth loop, and daily quests, all saved per player with DataStore session locking.

Global leaderboards

Ordered-data-store leaderboards for best distance, total coins earned, and rebirths, shown on in-world boards.

Anti-cheat & admin

Server-side distance validation that voids teleport exploits, plus an allowlisted admin dev panel for testing and support.

Custom physics, and keeping it honest

The heart of the game is the ball itself. It's built as two linked physics bodies: a collision shell that actually rolls and collides with the world, and an interior that holds the player's seat and is kept upright by an orientation constraint so the rider never tumbles while the shell spins. Movement isn't done by shoving forces around; the client sets a target angular velocity each frame and lets real physics, gravity, and the slope do the rest, which is what makes the momentum and terrain feel right. On top of that sit hybrid controls (auto-roll, strafe, a dedicated brake), a speed-reactive follow camera with bank tilt, and bubble durability that cracks in stages and ends in a real glass-shatter made of physics shards.

Because how far you roll decides your reward and your leaderboard rank, distance has to be trustworthy. The ball is network-owned by the player during a run for smooth control, so I validate distance on the server: a per-frame speed clamp absorbs one-off lag spikes but a run that keeps reporting impossible speed is voided with no reward, which shut down teleport and fling exploits in testing.

Around the run is a full collector economy: 100 config-driven Brainrots across five rarities with real animated models, a Tool-based inventory and base display, passive income, glass-strength / coin-multiplier / gem-luck upgrades, rebirth, daily quests, and global leaderboards, all saved per player with DataStore session locking and anti-dupe. It's built to run cross-platform on phone, tablet, PC, and console.
